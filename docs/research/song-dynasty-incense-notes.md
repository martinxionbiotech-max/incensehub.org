## Contradiction log

| Topic | Position A | Position B | Why they differ | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scale of Song aromatic trade | Popular retellings imply aromatics dominated state revenue | 宋史 figures are mixed-unit totals for a basket of goods, not a clean "incense tax" | Different accounting scope; court records bundle aromatics with pearls, ivory and gems | Report the figures with their units and caveats; do not compute a share of GDP |
| When the modern stick appeared | Many pages place 线香 in the Song | 香乘's Song-derived recipes are dominated by pills, cakes, powders and seal trails; extruded sticks are securely described in Ming sources | Retrospective labelling; "incense" is treated as one continuous product | Say the format is Ming; the Song contribution is heat control and formula literature |
| Origin of 香道 | Japanese kōdō is often treated as the parent tradition | Chinese heating method predates it and traveled east | Category mistake between the formal school and the technique | Chinese source, Japanese systematisation |
| Number of Song manuals | "Three great manuals" is common | 香乘 names four families and cites a ten-juan title now lost | Later canon-making; lost works | Present the canon as a later construction, not a contemporary list |

## Information-gain assets

1. A unit-aware reading of the 宋史 revenue lines (why 53万 is not a budget).
2. The "state monopoly → grading vocabulary" chain: 乳香 monopoly and 沉香 density grades both turn aroma into an administrable commodity.
3. A format audit: what Song recipes actually specify (pills, cakes, powders, seal trails) versus what modern shops call "Song-style".
4. A reconstruction of a Lin'an purchase, explicitly labelled as reconstruction.
5. A four-part test for "Song-style" marketing claims.
