## 1. Research question and editorial position

The market describes agarwood and sandalwood with one vocabulary — "grade", "wild", "sinking", "pure" — but the two materials are biologically and commercially different. The editorial position, formed before the source sweep, was:

> Agarwood is a wound response whose value is distorted by treatment; sandalwood is a slow-grown oil whose value is distorted by species substitution. Buyers should stop asking for a grade and start asking which claim is being made.

The research was then used to test that position, not to generate it.

## 3. Contradiction log

| # | Conflict | Why it exists | Resolution used in the pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| X-01 | Single fungus (*Phaeoacremonium parasitica*) vs mixed microbiome as the cause of resin | Early culture work isolated one organism; later community sequencing found diverse assemblages | Published as an open contradiction. Pages say the single-pathogen story is too simple, without claiming the community model is settled. |
| X-02 | "Sinking = best" vs reports of high-grade 奇楠 that half-sink | The water test measures average density; 奇楠's soft, evenly distributed resin can lower density | Split into two gauges: resin load vs fibre state. Neither is presented as a universal ranking. |
| X-03 | 奇楠 colour hierarchy: five distinct grades vs aging stages | Different historical texts and modern traders use the names differently | Published as traditional but partly disputed. The pages advise treating colour names as descriptive vocabulary. |
| X-04 | "Wild" premium vs plantation quality | Wild material is scarce and old; plantation material is younger but can be clean and consistent | Treated as separate axes (origin vs maturity), not a single quality ladder. |

## 4. Information gain (what the pages add beyond the top SERP results)

1. A two-gauge model that separates resin load from fibre state, with the water test's blind spot made explicit.
2. A five-stage formation clock (wound → colonisation → chemical response → maturation → death/burial) tied to the claims buyers actually see.
3. A four-component price model (mass, oil/resin content, provenance, craft) that turns "quality" into auditable questions.
4. A five-step authentication ladder ordered by cost and destructiveness (story → surface → water → heat → cut).
5. A formation-type table that treats 生结/熟结/虫漏/脱落 as context, not rank.
6. A species map that separates *Aquilaria*, *Gyrinops*, *Santalum*, and *Pterocarpus* — the red sandalwood trap.
7. A legality check in three parts (species, document, chain) that distinguishes CITES clearance from quality.
8. The "three ages" of sandalwood: tree age, log seasoning, oil oxidation — a storage framework the SERP usually omits.
